# Chapter 2 — Pack Math

Standing was the easy part.

The hard part was everything his body was still arguing about. Forearm. Ribs. Thigh. The Cur’s dying trickle had burned the edges shut into ugly half-seals, and every step cracked them open again. Blood-mud sucked at his boots. The snapped deadwood spear stuck to his palm where splinters had already made a home.

*Okay. Inventory: bleeding. Confused. Demon accountant in my head telling me to get up. Cool. Normal Tuesday.*

“Walk,” Mariusz said. Not tutorial-bright. Sharp enough to cut through the shakes. “Away from the smell. Carrion draws more than dogs.”

*Dogs. Right. That thing wasn’t a dog. Neither is whatever’s coming.*

Aeric walked.

The mist thinned into trees that had no business being this tall this close to… wherever the bus had been. Roots knuckled up through soil that looked freshly torn, like the ground had been stretched and never forgiven for it. The air still tasted copper-thick. Aether. He hated that he already had a word for it.

Distant screaming had gone patchy—less choir, more solo acts cutting short.

Analytical Eye kept trying to turn the forest into a diagram: sight lines, sound shadows, places a jointed thing could come from. It helped. It also made him notice the wet clicking sooner.

Not one set of legs.

Many.

---

He found the Weaver first.

Kid—sixteen, maybe seventeen—pressed back against a shattered trunk, hands shaking as threads of thick air spun between his fingers into crude luminous cords. Template work. Soft, fast, already fraying at the edges where panic chewed the formula.

Aeric knew the package. He’d rejected it in a white void that already felt like someone else’s dream.

**[Scan]**
> **Name:** Jonah Keller
> **Race:** Human
> **Level:** 1
> **Note:** Weaver Template *(Scaffolded)* — Non-hostile / Unstable channeling

*Jonah Keller. Level one. Soft ceiling with a name tag. Unstable. Yeah. No shit.*

The clicking resolved into shapes: carapace the color of old bruises, mandibles wet with something that had been a person twenty seconds ago. Three of them the size of motorcycles, low and wrong, venting Aether-stink from seams along their bellies.

**[Scan]**
> **Entity:** Aether-Warped Carrion Beetle
> **Level:** 3
> **Threat:** Pack scavenger — soft ventral seam; armor elsewhere

*Level three. Pack scavenger. Soft seam. Armor elsewhere. Eye’s doing homework while my thigh tries to quit.*

Jonah saw Aeric and made a sound that wanted to be a word and came out as a sob. “Help—please—I can—my Guardian said—”

One beetle lunged for Jonah’s cords. The weave caught a mandible, flared bright, and *popped* like wet string. Jonah screamed as feedback slapped up his arms in white sparks.

Mariusz’s voice cut in, clinical under the scrape. “Scaffolded channeling under panic. He’ll burn his wrists before he kills one. You have a spear and that hollow under your gut—your Dantian—half-seeded and hungry. Do the math.”

*Dantian. The battery. The thing that almost ate the Cur. Math. Always math with you.*

Aeric’s body already moved—not heroism, not mercy. The Eye had drawn a line from his spear tip to the nearest beetle’s pale ventral seam, and Stubborn Will had turned the scream of his own torn meat into background noise.

He went in low.

The first beetle turned too slow. Deadwood punched the soft seam under the thorax with a sound like cracking fruit. Ichor sprayed black-green and hot enough to blister. The thing shrieked in a register that made his teeth hurt and tried to fold him under a leg like a hydraulic press.

Aeric ripped the spear free, rolled—shin opening on a ridge of carapace—*fuck*—and drove the point again as the beetle reared. Soft seam. Same place. Deeper. He felt something give that wasn’t armor.

Null Cycling woke without asking.

Not the polite trickle from the Cur.

A *pull*—hungry, stupid, greedy—ripping at the beetle’s dying heat like his Dantian had grown teeth. Ambient Aether, hostile scrap, whatever slurry lived in the thing’s guts: it all tried to shove into him at once. Meridians that weren’t open screamed. The three cleared paths flared white-hot. The rest stayed clogged and punished him for wanting more.

It felt like swallowing broken glass and calling it dinner.

For half a second the pull found something denser in the corpse—a hard little knot under the ichor, like a seed—and Aeric’s gut *lunged* for it.

Then the knot slipped. Or he slipped. The Cycle choked, coughed sludge-taste up the back of his throat, and settled for burning raw fuel into the shin-rake and palm-splinters hard enough to cauterize without healing clean.

*What the fuck was that. That wasn’t cycling. That was—theft. Almost. Shit. I want it again. That’s bad. That’s useful. Same thing now?*

“Control it,” Mariusz snapped, and there was real anger under the scholar-voice—fear dressed as instruction. “You yank like a drowning man and you will rupture what little you’ve opened. Residue first. Foundations later. You are not ready to eat a core whole.”

*Core. He said core. File that. Also: drowning man. Accurate.*

The beetle shuddered and went still.

The other two did not care about Aeric’s personal growth.

They came together—pack math, not duel math—one high, one low, clicking in sync like they’d practiced on softer meat. Jonah’s cords flared again, wild, catching one mandible for a heartbeat. Noble. Useless. The second beetle smashed Jonah into the trunk hard enough that bark exploded and the luminous weave died like a blown fuse.

Aeric’s Eye did the cold thing.

Two beetles. Level three. Him: level one, half-butchered, spear slick, Cycle still hiccuping sludge. Save Jonah: die. Run: live. Maybe.

*He’s a person. He’s Jonah Keller. He’s a soft ceiling with a pulse. If I stay I become paste and Mariusz gets to find out what expire tastes like. If I go—*

The dark thrill rose anyway—wrong, awake, interested in the problem like it was a puzzle and not a murder.

He hated that it helped him move.

Aeric jammed the spear into the closest beetle’s eye-cluster—not a kill, a flinch—and sprinted the opposite vector from Jonah’s sobbing. Branches whipped his face. Pack clicks followed, then split: two staying with the easier meal that had already stopped weaving.

Behind him, Jonah made a sound that would live in Aeric’s teeth.

He didn’t look back.

*Coward. Survivor. Same résumé line today. Jonah Keller. Fuck. Fuck. Fuck.*

---

He ran until his lungs tried to climb out through his throat, then fell behind a root-wall big enough to hide a truck and bit his own wrist to keep from making noise.

Blood. Copper air. Distant feeding sounds—wet, rhythmic, done.

Mariusz was quiet long enough that Aeric almost filled the silence with something stupid.

Almost.

“Speak,” Mariusz said finally, “and I will answer one question that keeps you alive. Philosophy is a luxury for men not being hunted.”

Aeric spat ichor and laughed once, ugly. “How do I not die in the next hour.”

“Better. Open another meridian while your Cycle is still hot—or try. Three paths cannot carry what your Dantian wants to steal. Force a fourth. If you fail, you learn the shape of failure before a beetle teaches it permanently.”

*Always the encouraging type.*

Aeric sat with his back to the root, spear across his thighs, and reached for the hollow under his gut the way Mariusz had beaten into him in the white void. Null Cycling. Pull. Break down. Don’t be polite.

Ambient Aether scraped into him. The three open meridians took it like overloaded wires. He pushed for a fourth—found a seam of clog that felt like scar tissue wrapped around glass—and *shoved*.

Pain arrived with opinions.

Stubborn Will climbed the damage load and kept him conscious while the channel refused. Something in his chest hiccuped. The seam held. The Cycle stuttered and dumped heat into already-ruined muscle instead of progress.

He tasted blood that wasn’t from his mouth.

> **[ CYCLE AUDIT ]**
> **Attempt:** Meridian Opening — Channel 4
> **Result:** Failure — clog integrity unbroken
> **Note:** Forcing further risks micro-rupture. Stabilize. Hunt. Repeat.

“Failure,” Aeric panted. “Cool. Love that for us.”

Mariusz’s laugh was a dry rasp. “Good. Failure is data. Keller’s scaffold would have opened that channel for him and charged interest forever. You pay now. He—” A pause, thin. “He paid differently.”

*Jonah. Past tense. Yeah. I know.*

Aeric wiped his eyes with the back of a filthy hand and pretended it was sweat. The Eye kept replaying the soft seam, the denser knot he’d almost stolen, the way the pack had chosen the easier math without malice.

*Pack math. I did pack math too. I left Jonah.*

Shame sat in his gut beside the dark thrill and neither one won.

He pulled up the sheet because numbers were easier than the sound still stuck in his teeth.

“Overdraw impulse,” Aeric muttered. “So that’s what almost eating the knot was.”

“Almost,” Mariusz said. “Almost is the only reason you’re still a person with meridians instead of a smear with ambition. Next kill: take residue. Leave the core until I say otherwise. And if you meet another scaffolded child—”

Aeric waited.

Mariusz’s voice roughened, not soft. Never soft. Just honest in a way that hurt. “Do the math that keeps us both breathing. I have watched curiosity get people killed before. I will not romanticize your guilt while it does the same to you.”

*File that. Later. Survive first.*

Aeric stood on a ruined shin and a worse conscience, spear sticky, forest clicking somewhere far and not far enough.

*Learn it the hard way. Yeah. Hard way’s doing overtime.*

He moved deeper into the wrong trees—away from feeding sounds, toward denser mist and the next problem that wanted him soft.

Behind his eyes, Mariusz did not offer comfort.

He offered silence that felt like a man already counting costs he refused to name.
